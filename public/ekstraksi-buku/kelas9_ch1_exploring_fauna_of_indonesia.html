as Endangered by the IUCN due to mangrove deforestation.
Orangutan (Pongo pygmaeus / Pongo abelii): Orangutans are great apes native to the rainforests of Borneo
and Sumatra. Unlike Bekantans, orangutans do not have a tail, and they are much heavier (adult males weigh
up to 75–90 kg). They build nests high in the forest canopy from branches and leaves.

Indonesia is home to over 1.800 bird species, including the Helmeted Hornbill (Rangkong Gading – Rhinoplax
vigil) and the Cendrawasih (Bird of Paradise – Paradisaea apoda).
The Helmeted Hornbill is a very large forest bird native to Sumatra and Kalimantan. It has blackish-brown
feathers, a white belly, and two remarkably long central tail feathers that can reach up to 120 cm in length. Its
most distinctive feature is its solid red casque (helm gading) on top of its beak, which accounts for 11% of its
body weight! Male hornbills use this solid casque in mid-air head-to-head clashes to defend fruit trees. During
the nesting season, the female hornbill seals herself inside a high tree cavity using mud and fruit pulp, leaving
only a narrow slit through which the male feeds her and their chick for months. Tragically, the Helmeted Hornbill
is listed as Critically Endangered because it is poached for its solid casque.
Meanwhile, the Greater Bird of Paradise (Cendrawasih) is native to Papua and the Aru Islands. Male Cen-
drawasih birds are famous for their dazzling golden-yellow crown, emerald-green throat, and fluffy yellow-and-
white flank plumes used in elaborate courtship dances.

drawasih birds are famous for their dazzling golden-yellow crown, emerald-green throat, and fluffy yellow-and-
white flank plumes used in elaborate courtship dances.
